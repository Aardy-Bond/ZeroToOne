Cathal Rynne came back on the six o'clock and asked her, in the way of a man who
has been working up to a thing all afternoon, what was wrong with her engine.

"It coughs," Nuala said.

"It coughs going in and not going out."

"That's right."

"Then it isn't the engine," he said. "Let me see it in the morning."

He was there at seven with the canvas bag, which turned out to have tools in it,
and by half past nine he had the fuel line off and had shown her a length of
perished rubber with a crack in it you could put a fingernail into, and by
eleven the ferry took the eight o'clock run an hour late and did not cough at
all, going in or going out.

He would not take money. He said it twice and the second time he said it in a
way that closed the subject.

So Nuala took her father's watch off the inside of her wrist and put it into his
hand and folded his fingers over it, which was a thing she had not planned to do
and did not entirely understand having done.

"It runs four minutes fast," she said. "Don't have it seen to."

Cathal Rynne looked at it for a long moment. Then he put it on, on the inside of
his wrist, which she had not told him to do, and he went up the pier past the
rusting bicycle and out of Ardmore on the eleven o'clock bus.
