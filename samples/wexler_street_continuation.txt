INT. BASEMENT LAUNDRY ROOM - NIGHT

A single bulb swings. PRIYA stands at the dryer, folding a child's t-shirt.
The machine has been off for an hour. The clothes are still warm.

PRIYA
Okay. Okay, that's just the pipes. That's all that is.

From the far corner, a slow DRAG of something heavy across concrete. She does
not turn around.

PRIYA (CONT'D)
Arjun? Sweetheart, if that's you, this isn't funny.

The dragging stops. Directly behind her.

PRIYA (CONT'D)
I'm going to count to three. And then I'm going to turn around, and you are
going to be my son. One.

The bulb dies.

PRIYA (CONT'D)
Two.

Something breathes. It is not a child.

INT. KITCHEN - CONTINUOUS

DEV stands at the counter, phone face down. The basement door hangs open
behind him, a rectangle of black.

DEV
Priya? You want tea?

Nothing. He takes the first step down. The wood gives under him, wet.

ARJUN (O.S.)
Dad? Mum's down here. She wants you to come see.

Dev stops. His son is asleep upstairs. He put him there ninety minutes ago.

DEV
Arjun, stay where you are. Do not move.

ARJUN (O.S.)
She wants you to come see what she found.

INT. COUNTY RECORDS OFFICE - DAY

Dev sits across from MEERA, a records archivist, a box file open between them.

MEERA
The house on Wexler Street was built in 1911 by a shipping clerk named Aldous
Renn. The deed changed hands eleven times before your family bought it. The
consortium that held it through the twenties dissolved in 1931, following a
dispute over the eastern easement.

DEV
The eastern easement.

MEERA
Which had been surveyed incorrectly in 1908, and would be resurveyed twice
more before the matter was settled in county court in 1946. The 1946 ruling
is the one that matters, because it established the boundary that all
subsequent transfers relied upon. There were four such transfers between 1946
and 1971, each one recorded in a different clerk's hand.

DEV
Right.

MEERA
The 1971 transfer is where the paperwork becomes interesting. The grantor's
name appears three times in the ledger, spelled three different ways, which
was not unusual for the period but does complicate the chain of title
considerably.

INT. RECORDS OFFICE - LATER

The box file is emptier. Meera turns pages. Dev watches the clock.

MEERA
I should explain how the county organised its plat books, because it bears on
what you're looking at. Before 1954 the books were kept chronologically. After
1954 they were reorganised by parcel, which means anything before that date
has to be cross-referenced by hand against the index.

DEV
How long does that take?

MEERA
It depends on the decade. The forties are well indexed. The twenties are not
indexed at all. I've spent eleven years on this collection and I would not
call the twenties finished.

DEV
Ms. Meera. My wife has not spoken since Tuesday.

MEERA
I understand. I'm establishing context.

DEV
I don't need context. I need to know what is in my house.

INT. LUKE'S HOUSE - UPSTAIRS HALLWAY - NIGHT

Dev climbs the stairs with a torch. The beam finds Arjun's bedroom door, shut.

DEV
Arjun. I'm coming in.

He opens it. The bed is made. It has not been slept in.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
There is a particular arithmetic a parent does in the dark. Dev did it on the
landing, and the answer he got was that his son had been in the basement for
four days.

From downstairs, the dryer starts.

INT. BASEMENT - CONTINUOUS

Dev descends. The bulb is whole again, burning steady. Priya stands at the
dryer, folding a child's t-shirt.

DEV
Priya.

She turns. She is smiling, and it is her smile, and that is the worst part.

PRIYA
You came to see.

DEV
Where is he?

PRIYA
He's been helping me fold. He's very good at it now.

She holds up the t-shirt. It is the one she was folding four days ago. It is
still warm.

INT. BASEMENT - CONTINUOUS

Dev takes the shirt from her hands. Behind the dryer, the concrete is broken
open, a trough dug into it, roughly the length of a child.

DEV
Priya. What did you find down here.

PRIYA
The easement. It runs under the house.

She says it kindly, the way you explain something obvious to someone you love.

PRIYA (CONT'D)
It was surveyed wrong. All of it was surveyed wrong. The house isn't standing
where they think it's standing.

Dev looks into the trough. It has been dug from the inside.

ARJUN (O.S.)
Dad. Turn off the torch.

The voice is behind him, and below him, and it is very close.
