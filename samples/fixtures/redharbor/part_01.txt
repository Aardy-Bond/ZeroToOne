Redharbor sat under a winter sky the colour of wet slate. ELLA VENN had kept the
pilot's house at the end of the quay for nine years, since the night her brother
drowned off the bar and the harbour board asked her, as if it were a kindness,
to take his watch.

She kept one thing of his: a brass pocket compass with a cracked glass. It still
pointed true. She wore it on a cord inside her coat and never lent it, not even
to the skippers who knew her.

The hold of the coaster *Moth* was sealed. The harbour master, TOM BRIGGS, had
put his own padlock on it that morning and told Ella, in front of two witnesses,
that nobody opened that hold until the customs man came up from Whitby on Friday.

"Not you," Briggs said. "Not the crew. Not if the ship catches fire."

Ella asked him what was in it. Briggs said he did not know, and that was the
first time she had ever heard him admit ignorance of anything that floated.

She also asked who had the authority to seal a private coaster until Friday.
Briggs looked past her at the bar and did not answer. Who had ordered the seal,
and on whose paper, remained an open thing on the quay.
