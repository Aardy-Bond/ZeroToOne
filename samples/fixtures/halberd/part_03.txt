INT. DEV'S CAR — HALBERD STREET — NIGHT

Rain on the roof. Dev has a cassette recorder on the passenger seat, a cheap
one, and he is winding a tape back by hand with a pencil.

He presses PLAY.

VOICE (ON TAPE)
— and you tell Rennick the ledger goes in the river, not the fire. The fire
leaves ash and ash gets read.

Dev stops it. He rewinds four seconds and plays the same line again, and then
again, and on the third pass he says the word "Rennick" along with it, getting
the cadence of it, learning the shape of a man's mouth from eleven syllables.

DEV
(into the handset)
It's him. I've got him saying it.

VOICE (ON RADIO)
Saying it to who?

DEV
That's the part I don't have.

He looks up at the third-floor window. The light is on.

INT. HALBERD STREET — MARA'S FLAT — SAME

Mara sits with her back to the door and the tobacco tin unopened in her lap. She
has not played the tape tonight. She is listening to the rain and to a car
engine that has been idling in the street for forty minutes and has not gone
anywhere.

MARA
(to the door)
Go on, then.

INT. HALBERD STREET — STAIRWELL — LATER

Mrs Paris climbs to the third landing with a bucket she does not need, stops
outside Mara's door, and listens. Then she goes down again, and out, and across
the street to the car, and she taps on the glass.
