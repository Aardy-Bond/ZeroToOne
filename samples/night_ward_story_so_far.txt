INT. SHIRWAL COTTAGE HOSPITAL - NIGHT CORRIDOR - 2:40 AM

Rain on a tin roof. Somewhere a ceiling fan turns with a bearing gone dry.

REVATI, twenty-four, three weeks into her first posting, walks the corridor with a torch she does not need. At the far end there is a door. Green paint, an iron hasp, and a padlock the size of a fist.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Ward Four had been shut since the fire. Eleven months, and nobody had thought to take the sign down.

She puts her palm flat against the door. It is colder than the corridor.

MATRON SULOCHANA (O.S.)
That one stays shut.

Revati turns. SULOCHANA, fifty, is already dressed at this hour, which means she has not slept. A brass key hangs on a chain at her waist.

REVATI
Is there a patient in there?

SULOCHANA
There is nothing in there. That is why it stays shut.

She walks on. The key knocks against her hip with every step.

REVATI
Matron. Nobody has told me what happened.

Sulochana does not stop.

SULOCHANA
Dr. Anil Kadam died in Ward Four on the ninth of August. There is one key to that door and it is on this chain. Those are the two things you need, and you need neither of them tonight.

The corridor light stutters and holds. Revati stands where she is until the footsteps are gone.


INT. SHIRWAL COTTAGE HOSPITAL - RECORDS ROOM - DAY

Ledgers to the ceiling. Dust in the light from a single high window. Revati has the night admission register for August open across her knees.

She runs a finger down the ninth.

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Six patients were admitted to Ward Four that night. She counted them twice, because the second time she did not believe the first.

Five discharge entries, each in the same careful hand. Beside the sixth admission there is no discharge. There is a name, and the name has been scratched through so hard the nib has gone through the paper.

REVATI
Six in. Five out.

She holds the page to the window. Under the scratching, a shape that could be a letter and could be nothing.

GANPAT, the ward boy, seventy and unhurried, sets down a tray of tea.

GANPAT
That register should be in the cupboard.

REVATI
Who was the sixth?

Ganpat looks at the page for a long moment. Then he picks the tray back up, with the tea still on it, and leaves.


INT. SHIRWAL COTTAGE HOSPITAL - MATRON'S OFFICE - NIGHT

Sulochana sits with her hands flat on the desk. The register is in front of her, open to the ninth. She has been looking at it long enough that the tea beside her has stopped steaming.

Revati stands. Nobody has offered her a chair.

SULOCHANA
You went through the cupboard.

REVATI
I went through the register. It was not locked.

SULOCHANA
No. That was my mistake, not yours.

A silence. Rain again, lighter now.

SULOCHANA (CONT'D)
Anil went in that night to move a patient out of the smoke. He did not come back out. The board wrote it up as six admitted, five discharged, one deceased, and the deceased was the doctor. That is the version that exists.

REVATI
That is five discharged and a doctor. That is not six patients.

Sulochana closes the register. Then she reaches to her waist and unclips the chain, and the brass key comes away in her hand, and she sets it on the desk between them.

SULOCHANA
I have not opened that door in eleven months. I am too old to be the only one who knows what is behind it.

Revati does not move.

SULOCHANA (CONT'D)
Take it. Before I remember that I am your matron.
