INT. HALBERD STREET — MARA'S FLAT — NIGHT

A one-room flat above a shuttered chemist. MARA VOSS, 30s, sits on the floor
with a cassette recorder between her knees. She is winding the tape back by
hand, with a pencil, to save the batteries.

MARA
(to herself)
Once more and then I'll sleep.

She presses PLAY. A man's voice, distorted, mid-sentence.

VOICE (ON TAPE)
— and you tell Rennick the ledger goes in the river, not the fire. The fire
leaves ash and ash gets read.

Mara stops it. She takes the cassette out and holds it up to the bulb, as though
the tape itself might show her something.

MARA
One tape. No copies, no dubs, nothing in a bank. If this goes, it never
happened.

She puts the cassette in a tobacco tin and the tin inside the cistern of the
toilet, and she sits back down on the floor with her back against the door.

INT. HALBERD STREET — STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS

DEV OKONKWO, 40s, climbs three flights in the dark. He counts the landings under
his breath. At the third he stops and listens at Mara's door for a long moment,
and does not knock, and goes back down.

EXT. HALBERD STREET — NIGHT

Dev crosses to a parked car and gets in. A POLICE RADIO under the dash mutters
and he turns it down without looking at it.

DEV
(into a handset)
She's still there. No, she doesn't know me from the wall.

He watches the window. The light goes out.

DEV (CONT'D)
Somebody called this in three weeks ago. I'd like to know who.
